How the adaptivity works

Each section has two modules. Accuracy on Module 1 decides which form of Module 2 you receive — and only the harder form reaches the top of the scale.

Practical consequence: never rush Module 1 to bank time. Module 1 is not a warm-up; it is the gate.

Scoring mechanics

No negative marking. An unanswered question is a guaranteed zero, so every question gets an answer, flagged or not.

Raw score converts through a module-dependent scale, so the same number correct can yield different scaled scores.

On test day

Taken in Bluebook on a laptop or tablet. Exam setup must be completed in the app in the days before — skipping it can cost the sitting.

ID for India: valid passport. Also bring the printed admission ticket, a charged device and charger, pencil and scratch paper.

Section I · Reading and Writing

54 questions, 64 minutes, ~71 seconds each

Every question carries its own short passage of 25–150 words; there are no long shared passages. Domains appear in a fixed order on every test, and within each domain questions run easiest to hardest — except Standard English Conventions, which is sorted by difficulty alone with sub-types mixed.

1 · Craft and Structure

13–15 Q · ~28%
  • Words in ContextPrecise word choice; secondary meanings of familiar words
  • Text Structure and PurposeWhat a sentence or passage does, not what it says
  • Cross-Text ConnectionsHow a second author would respond to the first

2 · Information and Ideas

12–14 Q · ~26%
  • Central Ideas and DetailsMain idea; explicitly stated detail
  • Command of Evidence — TextualWhich quotation supports the stated claim
  • Command of Evidence — QuantitativeReading a table or graph to decide a claim
  • InferencesThe conclusion the text forces, not merely allows

3 · Standard English Conventions

11–15 Q · ~26%
  • BoundariesSentence joins, comma splices, colons, semicolons, supplements
  • Form, Structure and SenseSubject–verb agreement, pronouns, verb tense, modifiers, parallelism, apostrophes

4 · Expression of Ideas

8–12 Q · ~20%
  • Rhetorical SynthesisUsing bulleted notes to meet a stated writing goal
  • TransitionsNaming the logical relationship between two sentences
Section II · Math

44 questions, 70 minutes, ~95 seconds each

Roughly three-quarters multiple choice, one-quarter student-produced response. The Desmos graphing calculator and the reference sheet are available on every question — nothing here needs to be computed by hand out of principle.

1 · Algebra

13–15 Q · ~35%
  • Linear equations in one variable
  • Linear equations in two variables
  • Linear functions
  • Systems of two linear equations in two variablesConditions for none, one, infinitely many solutions
  • Linear inequalities in one or two variables

2 · Advanced Math

13–15 Q · ~35%
  • Equivalent expressionsFactoring, expanding, rational and radical rewrites
  • Nonlinear equations in one variable and systems in two variablesQuadratics, discriminant, extraneous roots
  • Nonlinear functionsQuadratic, exponential, polynomial, radical, rational; notation and transformations

3 · Problem-Solving and Data Analysis

5–7 Q · ~15%
  • Ratios, rates, proportional relationships and units
  • Percentages
  • One-variable data: distributions, centre and spread
  • Two-variable data: models and scatterplots
  • Probability and conditional probability
  • Inference from sample statistics and margin of error
  • Evaluating statistical claims: observational studies and experiments

4 · Geometry and Trigonometry

5–7 Q · ~15%
  • Area and volume
  • Lines, angles and trianglesSimilarity is the most-used unlabelled tool
  • Right triangles and trigonometrySOHCAHTOA, special triangles, sin/cos complements
  • CirclesEquation of a circle, arcs, sectors, inscribed angles, radians
