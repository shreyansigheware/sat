The premise

Above 1450, the test stops being about what you know.

Every strategy in this deck comes from the same finding: at the top of the scale you are not learning content, you are eliminating small repeatable mistakes.5 The March 2026 administration made the point plainly: an easier paper, and a harsher consequence for each slip.3

What recent papers emphasised

  • Multi-step word problems in real-world settings3
  • Inference and tone rather than recall
  • Transition questions with several plausible answers
  • Geometry built on angle relationships

The counter-intuitive part

An easier test is a more dangerous test. When more candidates score near the top, the scale tightens and a single careless error costs more than a hard question ever would.3

What that means for her

With board results already at this level, the content is not the constraint. Everything in this deck is about execution under a scale that punishes slips — which is the good news, because execution is trainable in three weeks and content isn't.

The single most-cited principle

A Module 1 mistake cannot be repaired later.

Both sections route you to an easier or harder second module based on Module 1 accuracy. Being routed to the easy Module 2 imposes a score ceiling that a perfect Module 2 cannot overcome.8 Perfect scorers therefore protect Module 1 accuracy above everything, including pace.5

What high scorers do differently in Module 1

  • Spend the time. Finishing Module 1 early buys nothing — the time does not carry overThere is no reward for speed in a module you have already answered
  • Verify the easy ones. Perfect scorers slow down on questions they already know how to solve, because that is where 750s lose points5
  • Re-read the stem before submitting — the wrong-quantity error is invisible to recalculation
  • Flag liberally, and use the full review page before the module closes

The asymmetry, stated plainly

Module 1 error → capped ceiling
Module 2 error → one question lost

These are not the same mistake. A rushed Module 1 to "save time for the hard module" is the most expensive habit a strong student can bring to this test — and it is the habit that JEE-style pacing actively trains.

Unlearn it deliberately. Practice tests are where that gets rewired, not test day.

What top scorers practise on

Official material only — and save the newest tests.

Bluebook, tests 4–11 free

Eleven full-length adaptive tests, in the real interface.4 Tests 1–3 were retired in Feb 2025 for being significantly easier than real administrations — a useful warning about what unrepresentative practice does to a predicted score.

Test 11 most closely reflects what is being administered now.4 Sequence accordingly: older tests early, newest ones last, when a realistic prediction actually matters.

Official Question Bank & Khan Academy

Retired real questions, filterable by domain and difficulty — the drilling tool. Khan Academy is the College Board partner course and generates targeted practice directly from Bluebook results.2

The two third-party exceptions

High scorers converge on the same two books, and only these two:9

  • The College Panda's SAT MathWritten by a perfect scorer; aimed squarely at the 800
  • Erica Meltzer — Reading & WritingThe grammar and rhetoric reference for advanced students

The technique that separates 750 from 800

Flag everything you are not certain of — including what you got right.

During the module

  • Answer every question — never leave a blank
  • Flag anything below 100% certainty, even when you have chosen an answer you believe in
  • Keep moving. A stubborn question costs three easy ones

In the time you reserved

  • Redo each flagged question from scratch, before looking at what you originally chose
  • If the fresh attempt disagrees with the first, that question was never actually solved — work it a third time

Afterwards — the part almost everyone skips

When reviewing a practice test, go through every question you flagged and every question you got wrong — so that even a question you guessed correctly gets reviewed.7

A right answer you were unsure of is an unearned point. It will not repeat. Under a scale where one raw mark is worth ten to twenty scaled points, those are exactly the questions that decide the sitting.3

right + certain → skip in review
right + unsure → review as if wrong
wrong → review, tag the cause
Math · what 800 scorers actually do

Slow down on the easy ones. That is the whole technique.

Where the points actually leak

Students chasing an 800 lose marks by rushing questions they already know how to solve. Perfect scorers slow down just enough to confirm what is being asked — especially on the easy items.5

Nobody reaches 780 and then fails to reach 800 because of a hard question. They fail because of an easy one.

Split the practice in two

  • Easy and medium → Desmos and plugging in answers6Speed and certainty, not elegance
  • Hard → learn the recurring patterns: quadratics, exponentials, circles6Recognition, not derivation

Desmos regression

Practice Tests 5 and 6 introduced regression-based Desmos questions, now considered a required skill rather than a nice-to-have.6

table → y1 ~ mx1 + b

Know it cold before test day. Full Desmos technique is on slide 3 of the Math deck.10

The counterweight Not every question wants Desmos. Solving without the calculator builds the intuition that tells you which questions want it — and typing a question in takes longer than solving it more often than students expect.5
Reading & Writing · what breaks scores

The section fails on time management, not comprehension.

Three habits that recur in every account

  • Read the question stem first. Passages are now 25–150 words, so the stem tells you what to look for before you spend the read8A digital-first habit — it was bad advice on the old paper test with its long shared passages
  • Master the recurring question types. Most questions fall into a small number of categories; mastering them covers roughly 80% of the section6
  • Skip hard questions deliberately. Flag and return — treating every question as equally worth your time is what runs the clock out6

Where recent papers turned hard

  • Dense passages needing careful interpretation — the reported difficulty spike of March 2026, and specifically a time problem3
  • Transition questions with several seemingly correct answers3Exactly why the R&W deck insists on naming the relationship before reading the choices
  • Writing was standard difficulty — it rewarded precision, not advanced knowledge3Which is the argument for front-loading the grammar rules
The one place R&W is kinder than Math Recent scales suggest R&W forgives more — around −2 raw still landing 760–780, against Math where −1 already costs you the 800.3 If time has to be traded between sections in the final week, Math precision is worth more per hour.
The habit every account names first

Log mistakes to find the pattern, not to collect the questions.

The error log is described as the single most powerful score-improvement tool: record every mistake after each session, then review the log weekly to spot patterns.4 The point is not the list. The point is the repeated cause the list exposes.

The patterns that actually turn up

  • Rushing Module 14The most expensive one, and the hardest to feel in the moment
  • Misreading question stems4Solved for x, the question wanted 2x
  • Consistently missing inference questions4A content pattern, and the one that needs drilling rather than a checking rule

Once a pattern is named, it gets a targeted drill or a written checking rule — not more mixed practice.

The log format

question · test & number
domain · which skill
cause · rule gap / misread /
  careless / timing / format
redo · date + 48h

Cause is the only column that matters. A log of topics tells you what to revise, which at this level is nothing. A log of causes tells you what to change, which is everything.

Weekly, not daily: patterns need volume before they are visible.

Operating rules

Rehearse the conditions, not just the questions.

Before the day

  • Take practice tests at the real start time, not late at nightPeak alertness is trainable and specific to the hour
  • Last full-length test three days out — late enough to be diagnostic, early enough to act on
  • Complete Bluebook exam setup in advance. It opens in the days before and skipping it can cost the sitting2
  • Passport, printed admission ticket, charged device and charger

During

  • Answer Eliminator on, at the first question1
  • Hide the timer; check at fixed checkpoints instead of continuously1
  • Protect Module 1 accuracy over pace, in both sections
  • Never a blank. Flag, guess, move
  • Reserve time to re-read stems, not to recalculate

The 10-minute break

It sits between the two sections. Eat something, stand up, do not review anything.

Math follows the break — the section where a single slip is most expensive. Arriving at it flat is a scoring decision, not a comfort one.

One sitting is not the plan Most 1500+ outcomes are built across two or three sittings with superscoring. 12 September is the diagnostic under real conditions; 3 October and 7 November are where the score is made. Registering for 3 October by 18 September matters, because the 12 September result does not arrive until 25 September.
